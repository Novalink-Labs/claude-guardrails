# Asking the user

When a skill needs a decision or a preference, offer choices instead of open questions. People answer faster, and the options show what's possible.

- **Use the AskUserQuestion tool when it's available:** up to four questions per call, two to four options each. Put the recommended option first, label it "(Recommended)", and say why in its description. The user can always type their own answer instead.
- **Offer "Decide for me"** where delegating makes sense (stack, library, fonts). A delegated choice is recorded as `Accepted (delegated)` with who delegated it and when.
- **Show visual choices.** For design variants, layouts, palettes and type, give each option a preview: a short token summary or a small ASCII sketch of the layout. Point to the rendered HTML previews as well.
- **Make options real and distinct,** each with its trade-off in one line. No filler options, and no option the team has already ruled out.
- **Ask only what the repo, the brief or earlier answers can't tell you.** Group related questions into one call rather than asking one at a time.
- **Without the tool,** ask the same questions as a numbered list with the recommendation marked, and accept a number or free text.
- **After the answer,** restate the decision in one line and record it where the skill says (an ADR, DESIGN.md or AGENTS.md).
